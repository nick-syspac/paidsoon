# Cash-flow forecasting with Xero data

Cash-flow forecasting is one of the most useful ways to make better decisions in a small business. It tells you whether money will be available when bills, payroll, rent, and taxes are due. If you already use Xero, you have a strong source of real financial data to build that forecast more accurately.

The challenge is not collecting the data. It is making sense of it before it becomes a problem. That is where PaidSoon helps. Instead of forcing business owners to read through invoices, payment timing, and outgoing costs in isolation, PaidSoon brings that Xero data into a clearer cash-flow view so the next decision is obvious.

## Why Xero data matters

Xero stores much of the information businesses need to forecast cash flow:

- unpaid invoices and their due dates
- bills due in the next 30, 60, or 90 days
- bank activity and reconciliation history
- payroll schedules
- tax obligations
- revenue trends over time

This matters because cash flow is not just about profit. A business can be profitable on paper but still experience a shortfall if cash is delayed, customers pay late, or bills are due before revenue lands.

The problem is that Xero data is often rich but fragmented. It is spread across invoices, bank activity, liabilities, and recurring costs. A business needs context to understand what is actually happening with cash, not just what the accounting system records.

PaidSoon helps translate that financial data into something more actionable: a view of what cash is likely to move, what gaps may appear, and what needs attention before the shortfall hits.

## What a helpful forecast should show

A useful cash-flow forecast usually combines three things:

1. opening cash balance
2. expected incoming cash
3. expected outgoing cash

From Xero, that usually means pulling together:

- customer invoices due soon
- cash receipts already recorded or expected from current receivables
- supplier bills due soon
- payroll runs and employee payments
- tax and BAS obligations
- loan repayments or financing costs
- planned expenses such as rent, software, and subscriptions

The key is to model the timing of each item, not just the total amount. A forecast is only as good as the dates attached to it.

PaidSoon focuses on this exact gap. It turns a large amount of Xero data into a usable forecast around the timing of cash inflows and outflows, making the picture easier to understand and act on.

## Start with the actual data you already have

The easiest way to build a forecast with Xero data is to begin with what is already known.

### 1. Review your receivables

Look at unpaid customer invoices and their due dates. Group them by expected payment window:

- due this week
- due in 30 days
- due in 60 days
- due in 90 days

This tells you which customer payments are likely to come in and when.

If your customer base pays slowly, your forecast should reflect that. If certain clients regularly pay late, you may need to add a delay or apply a conservative collection assumption.

PaidSoon helps make this easier by surfacing the likely cash timing behind those unpaid invoices instead of leaving business owners to manually estimate it.

### 2. Review your payables

Now check upcoming supplier bills and recurring costs. This includes:

- rent
- office costs
- software subscriptions
- contractor payments
- inventory purchases
- freight or delivery costs
- payroll and tax liabilities

This gives you a picture of expected outgoing cash. The more accurately you anchor this to Xero, the less likely you are to miss a short-term squeeze.

PaidSoon helps connect the outgoing obligations to your real cash situation, so you are not relying on a spreadsheet alone to figure out what is urgent.

### 3. Match the forecast to your real cash cycle

Many businesses track revenue in monthly reporting, but cash arrives differently. For example, a client may be invoiced in May and not pay until June. If your forecast only looks at revenue when it is booked, it will overstate cash.

That is why Xero data is so valuable: it helps you model the cash timing behind the reported numbers. PaidSoon takes that one step further by making those timing patterns easier to interpret and use in planning.

## Common forecasting mistakes

Even with good data, businesses still make predictable mistakes.

### Overestimating incoming cash

A business may assume all invoices will be paid on time, but not every customer pays on schedule. It is better to use a realistic collection assumption and update it as actual payment patterns change.

PaidSoon can help by making likely collection timing more visible before you rely on unrealistic assumptions.

### Ignoring payment timing

Revenue booked in one month does not necessarily equal cash in the same month. Forecasting must treat invoicing and collections separately.

### Forgetting irregular expenses

Large annual costs, tax payments, annual software contracts, and one-off supplier invoices can cause sudden cash shortages. These need to be added to the forecast, not buried in a monthly average.

### Building a forecast once and never updating it

A good forecast is a living tool. Update it weekly or at least monthly with actual Xero data so it reflects the business’s real position.

PaidSoon is designed for exactly this kind of ongoing monitoring. It keeps the forecast relevant as actual cash movements change.

## A simple forecasting approach

A straightforward method is to create a rolling 13-week or 90-day forecast. It is short enough to be practical and long enough to catch upcoming risks.

For each week or month, map:

- opening balance
- customer receipts
- supplier payments
- payroll
- loan obligations
- tax payments
- other planned outflows
- ending cash balance

This gives you a clear view of whether you are likely to stay above a safe cash threshold.

PaidSoon helps simplify this process by turning Xero inputs into a clearer, more actionable view of those weekly and monthly movements.
